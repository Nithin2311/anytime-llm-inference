\begin{abstract}
Standard autoregressive LLM inference executes every transformer layer for
every generated token, yielding per-token latencies that are highly variable
and potentially unbounded as context grows.
For latency-critical applications such as clinical decision support, this
tail behaviour threatens hard Service-Level Objective (SLO) deadlines in
ways that standard profiling cannot certify away.
We address this mismatch by casting LLM token generation as an
\emph{anytime algorithm} problem: rather than requiring all 22 transformer
layers to complete before committing a token, we exploit the predictive
signal available at intermediate layers to make deadline-aware routing
decisions entirely at the application level.

Three routers are implemented on TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat on a bare-metal
NVIDIA RTX~6000~Ada GPU (50.9~GB VRAM, CUDA~12.4):
(i)~a \emph{stateless two-pass} router that runs Layer~16 as a probe
     and falls back to the full 22-layer pass when confidence is low;
(ii)~a \emph{KV-cached single-pass} router that captures Layer-16 activations
     via a post-hoc forward hook during the contiguous 22-layer pass,
     eliminating KV-cache desynchronization entirely; and
(iii)~an \emph{async-overlap} variant that pipelines CPU tokenization with
      GPU inference via CUDA streams and pinned memory.

A heterogeneous pipeline decomposition establishes that GPU compute
dominates per-token latency ($T_{\text{gpu}}$~=~14.48~ms mean, 99.2\%
of total), leaving PCIe transfer (0.021~ms) and CPU preprocessing (0.093~ms)
negligible and confirming that application-level routing is the correct
abstraction.
At the operating deadline $D=45$~ms, the KV-cached router achieves
P99~TPOT~=~16.98~ms ($R = 0.377$) with 0\% deadline misses across
240~token samples; when the deadline tightens to $D=30$~ms the
stateless two-pass router fails with P99~=~30.47~ms and a 4.0\%
miss rate, while the KV-cached router's P99 of $\approx$20~ms
maintains 0\% misses---demonstrating that single-pass inference
is the schedulable design at tighter constraints.
An exit-layer ablation over L5--L20 confirms Layer~16 as the earliest
layer with substantial predictive signal (32\% oracle agreement, rising to
64.7\% for high-confidence tokens).
A 30-query PubMedQA clinical benchmark achieves 0\% deadline misses at
67.34~tok/s throughput and 71.4\% answer accuracy.
Gumbel EVT bounds establish $P(\text{TPOT}>45\,\text{ms})<10^{-6}$
for sequences up to 512~tokens, and thermal profiling over
500~consecutive tokens confirms WCET stability, validating the
static routing table.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Large language models have become foundational components in
latency-critical applications: clinical decision support~\cite{singhal2023large},
interactive human-computer interfaces~\cite{ouyang2022training}, and
autonomous vehicle reasoning~\cite{wen2023dilu}.
In all such contexts the system must commit a token---or produce an
answer---before a hard deadline, with no allowance for the tail latencies
that routinely arise in production deployments.
Unfortunately, standard autoregressive decoding executes all transformer
layers for every token and produces execution times that are both highly
variable and context-dependent, growing with the length of the KV cache.
There is no known closed-form bound, and empirical WCET measurements
alone provide only a single-sample guarantee.

This mismatch between standard inference and hard deadline requirements
is the central problem we address.
We adopt the \emph{anytime algorithm} paradigm~\cite{boddy1994deliberation,
zilberstein1996using}: a computation that can be safely interrupted at
any point and returns a valid (if approximate) result.
For transformer inference, the natural interruption point is at an
intermediate layer; a confidence signal derived from that layer's logits
then allows the router to decide whether the intermediate output is
sufficient for commitment, without issuing any additional forward calls.

A critical implementation challenge is KV-cache consistency.
Naively running a partial forward pass invalidates the cached key-value
states for later layers, corrupting subsequent tokens.
Our solution is a post-hoc \emph{forward hook} that captures intermediate
activations during the single contiguous 22-layer pass, giving both logit
vectors in VRAM before the routing decision is ever made.

Our system operates entirely in user space.
The router selects which logit vector to commit but neither preempts OS
threads nor controls GPU hardware scheduling.
The relevant metric is SLO compliance: whether
$\text{P99\_TPOT} \leq D$ across the inference workload.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Post-hoc forward-hook router (KV-cached):}
      A single-pass design using \texttt{register\_forward\_hook} on
      Layer~15 (0-indexed) captures the Layer-16 hidden state during the
      contiguous 22-layer pass. Both logit vectors are available in VRAM
      after one pass; the routing decision is post-hoc, requiring no
      second forward call and preserving KV-cache consistency for all layers.

\item \textbf{Async-overlap router:}
      CPU tokenization and H2D PCIe transfer are overlapped with GPU
      inference from the preceding token using CUDA streams and pinned
      memory, demonstrating a non-blocking pipeline architecture.

\item \textbf{Heterogeneous pipeline model:}
      Per-component latency decomposition $T_{\text{total}} =
      T_{\text{cpu}} + T_{\text{pcie}} + T_{\text{gpu}} + T_{\text{sync}}$
      directly motivates the application-level routing abstraction and
      bounds the maximum benefit of CPU-side optimizations.

\item \textbf{SLO compliance proof:}
      Empirical demonstration that $\text{P99\_TPOT} = 16.98$~ms
      at $D=45$~ms ($R=0.377$) with 0\% deadline misses across
      240~token samples on clinical prompts; and that the KV-cached
      design maintains 0\% misses even at $D=30$~ms where the
      stateless design fails (4.0\% miss rate).

\item \textbf{Exit-layer ablation (L5--L20):}
      Systematic comparison against the full-pass oracle validates
      Layer~16 as the earliest layer with meaningful predictive signal,
      offering a 26\% latency saving over the full pass at WCET.

\item \textbf{EVT probabilistic WCET bounds:}
      Gumbel EVT fits to 500 samples per cell establish
      $P(\text{TPOT}>45\,\text{ms})<10^{-6}$ for sequences $\leq 512$
      tokens, providing a statistically certified timing guarantee
      appropriate for clinical deployment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Anytime Algorithms}

Anytime algorithms~\cite{zilberstein1996using,boddy1994deliberation}
produce valid results at any interruption point, with quality improving
monotonically over time.
The scheduler monitors elapsed time and commits the best available result
when the deadline approaches, making this paradigm ideal for hard real-time
systems where a slightly lower-quality answer is always preferable to
missing the deadline entirely.
Our work applies this principle at the \emph{token level}: the router
observes the confidence of an intermediate-layer prediction and decides
in real time whether the full pass is necessary.

\subsection{Early Exit in Transformer Models}

Early exit in deep networks was introduced by
Teerapittayanon et al.~\cite{teerapittayanon2016branchynet} and later
adapted to transformers in FastBERT~\cite{liu2020fastbert} and
DeeBERT~\cite{xin2020deebert}, where confidence-triggered intermediate
exits reduce \emph{mean} inference cost.
Schuster et al.~\cite{schuster2022confident} extended this idea to
decoder-only models with confident adaptive language modeling.
LayerSkip~\cite{elhoushi2024layerskip} showed that predictive signal
in TinyLlama is concentrated in later layers, a finding our ablation
(Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) corroborates quantitatively.
Critically, prior work has focused on throughput and mean latency;
we instead target the \emph{tail} latency needed for hard SLO guarantees.

\subsection{Real-Time Scheduling for ML Inference}

Real-time scheduling of neural network inference has been studied in
embedded settings by Bateni and Liu~\cite{bateni2020neuos} and
Xiang et al.~\cite{xiang2019pipelined}, who address WCET analysis and
CPU/GPU pipeline partitioning for convolutional networks.
Our work extends this line of inquiry to the decoder-only LLM setting,
where token-level autoregression and KV-cache state introduce
challenges absent in fixed-topology feed-forward inference.
Crucially, our routing architecture requires no OS-level thread
preemption, making it portable to any environment where GPU compute is
accessible from a Python process.

\subsection{Probabilistic WCET}

Measurement-based probabilistic timing analysis~\cite{cucu2012measurement}
fits statistical distributions to execution time samples and certifies
tail latency at exceedance probabilities well below $1/n$.
Bernat et al.~\cite{bernat2002wcet} established Extreme Value Theory
(EVT), particularly the Gumbel distribution, as the appropriate model
for execution time extremes.
Davis and Cucu-Grosjean~\cite{davis2013quantifying} survey the state of
the art in pWCET analysis.
We apply Gumbel EVT to GPU forward-pass timing samples to derive
a statistically rigorous bound that is far stronger than the empirical
$\max \times 1.10$ heuristic.

\subsection{KV Cache Efficiency}

PagedAttention~\cite{kwon2023efficient} and FlashAttention~\cite{dao2022flashattention,
dao2023flashattention2} are the primary techniques for reducing KV-cache
memory and recomputation cost in production serving systems.
We exploit PyTorch~2.4's scaled-dot-product attention (which invokes
FlashAttention kernels implicitly) and rely on the $O(n_{\text{cache}})$
single-token pass structure to bound per-token latency
independently of accumulated context length---a key property for
schedulability analysis.

\section{System Architecture}

\subsection{Hardware and Model}

\textbf{Hardware:}
Bare-metal NVIDIA RTX~6000~Ada Generation (50.9~GB GDDR6, CUDA~12.4,
Ada Lovelace architecture, 18{,}176 CUDA cores).
Bare-metal deployment is essential for valid WCET measurements;
hypervisor-induced scheduling jitter would corrupt worst-case timing
estimates in ways that are impossible to characterize post-hoc.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Model:}
TinyLlama\slash{}TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat-v1.0~\cite{zhang2024tinyllama}: 22
transformer decoder layers (LlamaDecoder), hidden dimension 2{,}048,
32 attention heads, vocabulary 32{,}000, \texttt{bfloat16} precision.
Chat-template formatted prompts are used throughout so that measured
latencies reflect deployment-realistic context lengths
($\approx$95--145~tokens); using raw text would underestimate the context
overhead present in real clinical queries.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Framework:}
PyTorch~2.4.1+cu124, HuggingFace Transformers~5.6.0.

\subsection{EarlyExitTinyLlama and the Post-Hoc Hook}

The core architectural challenge is capturing an intermediate-layer
prediction without disrupting the contiguous forward pass that populates
the KV cache.
We solve this with \texttt{EarlyExitTinyLlama}, a wrapper that registers a
\texttt{forward\_hook} on \texttt{model.layers[15]} (0-indexed, i.e.\
Layer~16) before every \texttt{forward\_cached()} call:

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE $\text{captured} \leftarrow \{\}$
\STATE \textbf{def} $\text{hook}(\text{module}, \text{in}, \text{out})$:
\STATE \quad $\text{captured}[\texttt{"h16"}] \leftarrow \text{out}[0]$
       \COMMENT{GPU ref, no host copy}
\STATE {\small \texttt{handle} $\leftarrow$ \texttt{layer15.register\_forward\_hook(hook)}}
\STATE $\text{out} \leftarrow \texttt{base\_model}(\text{ids},\, \text{past\_kv})$
       \COMMENT{22 layers run}
\STATE $\ell_{22} \leftarrow \text{out.logits}$
\STATE $\ell_{16} \leftarrow \texttt{lm\_head}(\texttt{norm}(\text{captured}[\texttt{"h16"}]))$
\STATE \textbf{return} $\ell_{16},\, \ell_{22},\, \text{out.past\_key\_values}$
\end{algorithmic}

The hook stores only a GPU tensor reference---no host transfer, no
synchronization.
All 22 layers execute contiguously; RMSNorm and the LM head are applied
strictly after \texttt{base\_model()} returns, so the hook captures
pre-norm hidden states without altering the computation graph.
Most importantly, every layer writes its KV states on every token
regardless of which logit vector the router ultimately commits.
This eliminates KV-cache desynchronization, the fundamental flaw of
two-pass stateless designs.

\subsection{Three Application-Level Routers}

\subsubsection{Stateless Two-Pass Router}

The stateless router (\texttt{generate\_stateless\_anytime}) serves as
our schedulability baseline.
It first runs Layer~16 as a lightweight probe; if the resulting
confidence is high enough, it commits the intermediate logit and skips
the full pass.
When confidence falls below a linearly decayed threshold, the full
22-layer pass is issued:
\begin{equation}
\theta_t = \theta_{\min} + (\theta_{\max} - \theta_{\min})
           \cdot \frac{r - w}{D - w}
\label{eq:decay}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is remaining budget, $w = \text{WCET}_{\text{safe}}(\text{seq\_len})$,
$\theta_{\max}=0.8$, $\theta_{\min}=0.3$.
As we show in Section~\ref{sec:comparison}, the two-pass nature of this
design makes it unschedulable at $D=30$~ms for typical chat-template
prompts: when 89.3\% of tokens require the second pass, the combined
P99 reaches 30.47~ms, exceeding a 30~ms deadline by 1.6\%.

\subsubsection{KV-Cached Single-Pass Router}

The KV-cached router (\texttt{generate\_anytime\discretionary{\_}{}{\_}with\_kv})
addresses the schedulability failure directly.
Because both logit vectors are already in VRAM after the single forward
pass, the routing decision reduces to a pure confidence threshold check:
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE $\ell_{16}, \ell_{22}, \text{kv}' \leftarrow
       \texttt{forward\_cached}(\text{ids},\, \text{past\_kv})$
\STATE $c \leftarrow \max(\texttt{softmax}(\ell_{16}[-1]))$
\IF{$c \geq 0.55$}
    \STATE commit $\arg\max(\ell_{16}[-1])$ \COMMENT{Early Exit (Thresh)}
\ELSE
    \STATE commit $\arg\max(\ell_{22}[-1])$ \COMMENT{Full Pass}
\ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}

No forced-exit logic is required: the single-pass always completes
within the deadline budget, so the routing branch is a post-hoc
quality selection rather than a timing intervention.
The threshold check adds zero GPU compute overhead because both vectors
are already resident in VRAM before the branch executes.

\subsubsection{Async-Overlap Router}

The async-overlap router (\texttt{generate\_anytime\discretionary{\_}{}{\_}async\discretionary{\_}{}{\_}overlap})
pushes the pipeline model to its logical limit by overlapping CPU
preprocessing of token $t{+}1$ with GPU inference of token $t$:

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Phase~A} (CPU): write $\text{tok}_{t+1}$ to pinned buf;
       \texttt{.to("cuda", non\_blocking=True)}
\STATE $\text{stream.wait\_stream}(\text{current\_stream})$
       \COMMENT{DMA lands before GPU reads}
\STATE \textbf{Phase~B} (GPU): launch \texttt{forward\_cached}
       on inference stream (non-blocking)
\STATE \textbf{Phase~C} (CPU): overlapped decode/record of token $t$
\STATE $\text{stream.synchronize}()$
       \COMMENT{wait for GPU result}
\STATE \textbf{Phase~D}: post-hoc routing on $\ell_{16}, \ell_{22}$
\end{algorithmic}

The pipeline model (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) shows that
$T_{\text{cpu}} = 0.093$~ms is only 0.64\% of the token budget,
so the overlap saves $\approx$0.09~ms mean latency.
On the current hardware the benefit is modest, but the architecture
is the correct foundation for deployments where CPU preprocessing
is heavier (e.g., retrieval-augmented generation) or where PCIe
bandwidth is a bottleneck.

\subsection{WCET Table and Admission Policy}

Both KV-cached routers load a per-sequence-length WCET table from
\texttt{wcet\_results.json} at startup and apply a conservative
$1.10\times$ safety margin:
\begin{equation}
\text{WCET}_{\text{safe}}(s) = 1.10 \times
    \max\bigl\{\text{WCET}_{\text{measured}}(s_i) : s_i \geq s\bigr\}
\label{eq:wcet_safe}
\end{equation}
The ceiling lookup (smallest profiled bin $\geq$ current sequence length)
ensures the bound is always conservative rather than interpolated.
A request is admitted only if $\text{WCET}_{\text{safe}}(s) \leq D$,
rejecting sequences whose WCET cannot fit within the deadline before
any computation begins.

Layer~16 occupies the sweet spot: captured for free during the full pass

critical under tighter constraints, as the three-way comparison and

architecture remains schedulable.

\subsection{EVT Probabilistic WCET Bounds}
\label{sec:evt}

The empirical WCET ($\max \times 1.10$) computed from 50 runs is a
useful engineering heuristic but provides no probabilistic guarantee
below exceedance probability $1/n = 0.02$.
In a clinical deployment, a one-in-fifty chance of exceeding the bound
is unacceptably weak.
Extreme Value Theory~\cite{bernat2002wcet,coles2001introduction}
addresses this by extrapolating the timing tail to exceedance
probabilities far below $1/n$ using a fitted statistical model.

We collect 500 timing samples per (seq\_len, exit\_layer) cell and fit
a Gumbel (Type~I EVT) distribution to the upper 20\% of samples
(Peaks-Over-Threshold method).
The Gumbel CDF is
$F(t) = \exp\!\bigl(-\exp(-(t-\mu)/\sigma)\bigr)$
with parameters estimated by maximum likelihood.
The probabilistic WCET bound at exceedance probability $\varepsilon$ is
$\text{pWCET}(\varepsilon) = F^{-1}(1-\varepsilon)$.

domain.

\subsection{Multi-Request Capacity Analysis}
\label{sec:capacity}

Under a token-level round-robin policy serving $N$ concurrent inference
requests, the effective TPOT seen by each request scales as $N \times C$,
where $C$ is the per-token execution time.
The system meets the SLO for all $N$ requests when $N \times C \leq D$:
\begin{equation}
N_{\max} = \left\lfloor \frac{D}{C} \right\rfloor
\end{equation}

Using the benchmark P99 TPOT $C = 20.69$~ms at $D = 45$~ms:
\begin{equation}
N_{\max} = \left\lfloor \frac{45}{20.69} \right\rfloor = 2; \quad
N{=}2: \; 2 \times 20.69 = 41.38\;\text{ms} \leq D
\end{equation}

for system operators scaling this architecture.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why KV Caching Is Essential}

The stateless two-pass router is not simply suboptimal---it is
architecturally incompatible with hard deadline guarantees at tight
scheduling constraints.
For chat-template PubMedQA prompts ($\approx$150~tokens), the L16 probe
consumes $\approx$10.6~ms; when confidence falls below threshold
(89.3\% of tokens), the subsequent full pass adds another $\approx$14.4~ms.
The resulting mean of 23.2~ms and P99 of 25.3~ms leave only 44\%
headroom at $D=45$~ms, and when the deadline tightens to $D=30$~ms
the P99 of 30.47~ms crosses the line with a 4.0\% miss rate.
No threshold tuning can fix this: the miss rate arises from
the two-pass architecture itself, not from threshold miscalibration.

The KV-cached design resolves this by collapsing two passes into one.
The forward hook stores a GPU tensor reference to the L16 hidden state
at effectively zero cost: no host transfer, no synchronization barrier,
no extra kernel launch.
After the contiguous 22-layer pass returns, both logit vectors are
already in VRAM and the routing decision is a pure Python branch.
The result is a 37\% reduction in mean TPOT (23.2~ms to 14.5~ms) and
0\% deadline misses at both $D=30$~ms and $D=45$~ms.

\subsection{GPU Dominance and the Right Level of Abstraction}

The pipeline decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) carries a
design lesson that generalizes beyond this specific system.
When 99.2\% of latency resides in GPU compute, the effective control
surface for a real-time scheduler is the GPU computation itself---not
thread priority, CPU preprocessing, or PCIe transfer optimization.
We confirmed this empirically: elevating CPU process priority via
\texttt{SCHED\_FIFO} produced $<0.1$~ms change in P99, indistinguishable
from measurement noise.
The async-overlap router demonstrates the correct handling of the
remaining 0.7\%: pipeline it entirely, make it invisible.
But the primary lever remains the routing decision that determines
which GPU-computed logit vector to commit.

This observation should inform architecture decisions at larger model
scales.
For a 70B model on multi-GPU infrastructure, $T_{\text{gpu}}$ will
dominate even more strongly, and CPU-side scheduling policies will be
correspondingly less relevant.
The application-level hook approach remains the right abstraction
regardless of model size.

\subsection{EVT Framing and Appropriate Confidence Levels}

The pWCET analysis targets exceedance probabilities in the
$10^{-3}$--$10^{-6}$ range, which is appropriate for clinical decision
support~\cite{singhal2023large}.
For context, aviation certification (DO-178C) demands $10^{-9}$, and
automotive safety (ISO~26262, ASIL-D) demands $10^{-8}$.
A biomedical question-answering system sits closer to IEC~60601-1 general
medical device requirements, where $10^{-3}$--$10^{-4}$ exceedance
is the relevant benchmark.
Our empirical result ($P(\text{miss}|D{=}45\,\text{ms}) \ll 10^{-3}$
for seq$\leq$512) is conservatively strong for this domain, providing
a comfortable safety margin without over-engineering for aviation-grade
requirements that would be disproportionate to the application risk profile.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

\textbf{Model scale:}
TinyLlama-1.1B is the smallest production-quality LLM available, chosen
to enable rapid iteration on the routing architecture.
Larger models (7B, 70B parameters) will have substantially higher
per-token latency and different layer-signal profiles; the framework
scales in principle, but the WCET table and optimal exit layer must be
re-profiled for each model family.

\textbf{Exit head quality:}
The shared LM head is applied at Layer~16 without any task-specific
fine-tuning.
Training a lightweight dedicated exit head (a small MLP or even a
learned linear probe) would likely push L16 oracle agreement well beyond
32\%, increasing the voluntary early-exit rate from 5.3\% and pulling
the tail distribution down further without sacrificing output quality.

\textbf{Multi-request batching:}
The round-robin $N_{\max}=1$ capacity limit is conservative and reflects
the absence of hardware-level batching.
Serving multiple requests in a single GPU kernel invocation could raise
this substantially---though at the cost of re-profiling the WCET table
under batch load, since batched execution has different memory bandwidth
characteristics.

\textbf{Async benefit at scale:}
The async-overlap router's 0.09~ms saving would grow proportionally
with $T_{\text{cpu}}$ in heavier preprocessing pipelines (e.g.,
retrieval-augmented generation with embedding lookups), or on PCIe~3.0
systems where $T_{\text{pcie}}$ is larger.
The current implementation is the correct architectural foundation for
those scenarios.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We presented a KV-cached anytime algorithm for LLM token generation that
enforces hard SLO deadlines by design, without modifying model weights,
without OS-level thread preemption, and without sacrificing output quality.
The central insight is that the KV-cache desynchronization problem inherent
in two-pass designs disappears when intermediate activations are captured
via a post-hoc forward hook during the single contiguous forward pass:
both logit vectors arrive in VRAM together, and the routing decision is
a zero-cost post-hoc branch rather than an architectural bottleneck.

On a bare-metal RTX~6000~Ada GPU, this design achieves P99~TPOT = 16.98~ms
at $D=45$~ms ($R=0.377$) with 0\% deadline misses across 240~token samples.
The stateless two-pass router, while also meeting $D=45$~ms, fails when the
deadline tightens to $D=30$~ms---P99 rises to 30.47~ms with a 4.0\%
miss rate---while the KV-cached design maintains 0\% misses even at
$D=30$~ms, demonstrating the architectural advantage of the single-pass
design.
An async-overlap variant lowers P99 to 16.81~ms by correctly pipelining
all CPU-side work behind GPU inference.
A 30-query PubMedQA benchmark confirms 0\% deadline misses at
67.34~tok/s throughput and 71.4\% answer accuracy under deployment-realistic
conditions.

The heterogeneous pipeline decomposition shows that 99.2\% of per-token
latency is GPU compute, directly validating application-level routing as
the right abstraction and ruling out OS scheduling or PCIe optimizations
as meaningful levers.
Gumbel EVT fits to 500 samples per cell certify $P(\text{TPOT}>45\,\text{ms})<10^{-6}$
for sequences up to 512~tokens, providing a statistically grounded timing
guarantee beyond what empirical worst-case measurement can offer.
Thermal profiling over 500 consecutive tokens confirms that GPU latency
is stable under sustained load, validating the static WCET routing table.

Taken together, these results establish a practical and principled path
toward schedulable LLM inference for latency-sensitive clinical
applications---one that requires only a hook registration and a
confidence threshold, not a new training recipe or custom GPU kernel.
